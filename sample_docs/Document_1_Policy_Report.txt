# Global AI Regulation: Policy Report 2026

## Overview
The landscape of Artificial Intelligence regulation is rapidly maturing, globally driven by the passing of the landmark EU AI Act. This report outlines the global standards being negotiated in 2026.

## Risk Classifications
The EU AI Act classifies AI systems according to the risk they pose to fundamental rights:
1. **Unacceptable Risk:** These AI systems are strictly prohibited. These include social scoring systems by governments and real-time biometric categorization in publicly accessible spaces. According to the Act, *there are absolutely no exceptions for real-time biometric categorization* as it constitutes a severe violation of privacy.
2. **High Risk:** Includes AI used in critical infrastructure, education, employment, and law enforcement. These systems require strict conformity assessments before market entry.
3. **Limited Risk:** Chatbots and deepfakes fall here. They are subject to transparency obligations (e.g., users must be informed they are interacting with an AI).
4. **Minimal Risk:** Spam filters and video games. Not regulated.

## Fines and Enforcement
Non-compliance under the EU AI Act can result in substantial penalties. The maximum fine for violating prohibited AI practices is up to €35 million or 7% of a company’s worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher.

## Impact on Open-Source
A key debate has centered on open-source foundation models. The policy report indicates that open-source models must still adhere strictly to the transparency rules if they surpass the compute threshold for systemic risk (greater than 10^25 FLOPs). Fully exempting open-source could create massive loopholes.
